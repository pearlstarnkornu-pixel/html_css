About Me

I have spent the last eight years photographing the deserts, canyons, and monsoon storms of the American Southwest. What started as a hobby on weekend hikes turned into a small print business after a local gallery asked to show a handful of my sunrise shots from Saguaro National Park.

I shoot mostly at dawn and dusk, when the desert light is softest, and I try to visit every location at least twice before calling a shot finished.

"Marisol has an eye for the moment just before the light changes — her prints hang in three galleries across the state."

Quick Tip

If you only remember one thing from my workshops: scout your composition the evening before, then return at first light. Half of a good desert photo is knowing exactly where to stand.

Experience & Skills

  • Long-exposure and night-sky photography
  • Studio and on-location print retouching
  • Leading small-group photo hikes since 2022
  • Featured in Southwest Lens Magazine, 2024 and 2025

Before photography became a business, I worked as a (mostly unrelated) park ranger for four years, which is where I first learned to read desert weather patterns — a skill that still shapes when and where I shoot today.

Interests & Hobbies

Outside of photography, I restore old film cameras, volunteer with a local trail-maintenance crew, and I am slowly working through every named peak within two hours of Tucson.

Current Gear

Full-frame mirrorless body, a 16–35mm wide-angle lens for landscapes, and a sturdy carbon-fiber tripod that has survived more wind than I expected it to.
