Why I Finally Switched to a Mechanical Keyboard

By Dara Whitfield — August 14, 2026

For nine years I typed on whatever laptop keyboard came in the box. It never occurred to me that the thing my fingers touched for eight hours a day was worth thinking about, until a coworker let me try her mechanical board during a slow afternoon.

The difference was immediate. Each keypress had a distinct point where it registered, instead of the mushy, uncertain travel I was used to. I made it through an entire paragraph without a single typo, which for me is genuinely rare.

Choosing a Switch Type

Mechanical keyboards are built around swappable switches, and the switch you pick changes the whole feel of typing. I tried three before settling on one:

Linear switches move straight down with no bump, which suits fast typists but offers little feedback. Tactile switches have a small bump partway through the press, letting your finger feel confirmation without bottoming out. Clicky switches add an audible click on top of that bump — satisfying at home, less so in a shared office.

I landed on tactile switches. They gave me the feedback I wanted without the noise complaints from my roommate.

What Actually Changed Day to Day

Six months in, the biggest change was not speed but comfort. My wrists ache less at the end of a long writing session, and I catch myself typing longer stretches without checking the screen for mistakes.

It also made me more deliberate about my whole desk setup. Once you notice how much one small upgrade changes daily comfort, it is hard not to look at everything else with the same question in mind.
